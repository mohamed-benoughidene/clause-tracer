How a review works

  1. Load a contract and pick a playbook.
  2. Check the split and the private details.
  3. The app labels each position and shows its evidence.
  4. You check every label: confirm it or change it.
  5. Write a message to the other side, or save a report.

Words used in this app

There are four labels, plus grey for parts no position covers. Each is shown as a stamp.

Preferred
The clause gives you at least what your playbook says you want.
Acceptable
Not as good as preferred, but inside the limit you set in your playbook.
Walk-away
Past the limit you set in your playbook. Ask for a change before you sign.
Unsure
The wording is too unclear to label, or the quote couldn't be confirmed. The app always says why. You decide.
Not covered
No position covers this part.

Legal terms in plain English

Indemnity
A promise to pay the other side's costs if something goes wrong, such as a claim from a third party. Check who promises what.
Limitation of liability
A cap on how much one side can owe the other if things go wrong. “Uncapped” means there is no limit.
Governing law
Which country's or state's law decides what the contract means, and where a dispute is heard.
Termination
Ending the contract. Check how much notice each side must give, and whether you're paid for work already done.
Confidential information
Anything you must keep private. Check what counts and how long the promise lasts.
Intellectual property (IP)
Who owns the work you make: you, or the other side. Check when it passes to the other side: when you make it, or once you're paid.
Work made for hire
A rule that the other side owns what you create from the moment it exists.
Non-compete
A promise not to work for competitors for a set time after the contract ends.
Warranty
A promise that the work meets a standard. Check what you're promising, and what happens if the work falls short.
Force majeure
Events nobody controls, such as floods or strikes, that excuse a side from its duties.

What gets sent

Only your positions and the contract go to OpenRouter, with names swapped for placeholders like “Person 1”. Never playbook names, customers, your notes, or To and From names. No telemetry.
